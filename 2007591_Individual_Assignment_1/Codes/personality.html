Edward A. Murphy Jr

"Anything that can go wrong, will go wrong" -Murphy 1949

Edward A. Murphy Jr. is a role model for me because of his contributions to engineering and his approach to problem-solving. He is best known for Murphy’s Law, which emphasizes that engineers should consider what could go wrong when designing a system. I admire this mindset because it encourages me to think critically, identify potential problems, and find solutions before they become serious issues. As someone pursuing a career in software development, I believe this way of thinking is important because software engineers must anticipate errors and build reliable systems.

“LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and CoPilot must be used as a tool not a crutch”.
Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot have changed the way students can access information and receive assistance. The statement that “LLMs must be used as a tool, not a crutch” means that students should use artificial intelligence to support their learning rather than depend on it to do their academic work for them.

Using LLMs for Programming

As a computing student, I believe LLMs can be especially useful when learning programming. For example, if I am struggling to understand a programming concept, I can ask an LLM to explain it in simpler terms or provide a basic example.

Exam Preparation and Research

LLMs can also support exam preparation and research. I can use an LLM to generate practice questions, explain topics from my lecture notes, summarize difficult concepts, or identify areas I need to study further.

The Danger of AI Dependence

The biggest danger is becoming dependent on AI. If I use an LLM to write every assignment, solve every programming problem, or answer every question, I may produce good-looking work without actually developing the knowledge and skills that university is supposed to teach me.

Responsible Use

Where and when I use LLMs matters. I can use them during personal study sessions, while reviewing lecture material, while practicing programming, and while researching ideas.

Conclusion

Ultimately, I believe the statement “LLMs must be used as a tool, not a crutch” is about balancing technology and human ability. LLMs can make learning more accessible and provide useful assistance, but they should not replace the effort required to learn.
